# Platform promotion rules

What each network's own policy says about running a giveaway on it. All twelve were read on 9 September 2026 and the lines below quote or closely paraphrase the pages. Policies change, and TikTok has already announced a Community Guidelines update for 24 September 2026. Re-read the page before launch, every time.

The five big networks come first, then the rest in order of how often they appear as entry actions in the campaign data.

## Facebook (Meta Pages, Groups and Events Policies, Promotions section)

- The promoter must "ensure that your promotion complies with applicable legal and regulatory requirements", with the examples of providing official rules, disclosing offer terms and eligibility, and registering the promotion with the relevant authorities.
- Participants must "acknowledge that the promotion is in no way sponsored, endorsed, administered by or associated with Meta."
- Promotions "must not require or incentivise participants to share, repost, tag others or in any other way publicise your promotion."
- "Meta will not assist you in administering a promotion."

What it means: on Facebook, share-to-enter, tag-a-friend and extra entries for sharing are out. Ask for a like, a comment, a page visit, or send people to your own entry page.

Source: https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/pages_groups_events

## Instagram (Promotion guidelines, Instagram Help Centre)

- If you use Instagram to communicate or administer a promotion, you are responsible for its lawful operation: the official rules, offer terms and eligibility requirements (age and residency among them), and compliance with the rules and regulations governing the promotion and its prizes, including registration and regulatory approvals.
- You must not inaccurately tag content or encourage users to inaccurately tag content, with the example of tagging themselves in photos they are not in.
- Promotions must include a complete release of Instagram by each entrant and an acknowledgement that the promotion is in no way sponsored, endorsed or administered by, or associated with, Instagram.
- Instagram will not assist in administering the promotion and cannot advise on consent for use of user content. Using the service to administer a promotion is at your own risk.
- A Brazil-specific line: promotions communicated or managed on Instagram in Brazil must comply with the Secretariat of Prizes and Bets (SPA) in the Ministry of Finance.

What it means: tag-a-friend is allowed only where the tag is accurate. Do not ask people to tag themselves or others in content they are not part of. Put the release and the "not sponsored by Instagram" line in the terms.

Source: https://help.instagram.com/179379842258600

## X (Guidelines for Promotions on X, X Help)

- Discourage the creation of multiple accounts. Include a rule that anyone found using multiple accounts to enter is ineligible, since X may suspend all of those accounts.
- Discourage posting the same post repeatedly. Duplicate or near-duplicate posts violate the X Rules and can get entrants filtered out of search. X recommends stating that multiple entries in a single day will not be accepted, and warns against mechanics like "whoever reposts this the most wins".
- Ask people to mention you in their post so you can see every entry in Notifications, because a public search may not show all of them.
- Encourage hashtags relevant to the contest. Adding your hashtag to unrelated posts can violate the X Rules.
- Follow the X Rules and search best practices, and comply with applicable laws. X says compliance is your responsibility and suggests consulting an attorney.

What it means: one entry per person per day, a mention, no bare repost counts, and no "most reposts wins" mechanics.

Source: https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-contest-rules

## YouTube (contest policies and guidelines)

- Creators are solely responsible for the contest and it must comply with all applicable laws.
- Official rules must state that YouTube is not a sponsor and require entrants to release YouTube from liability, link to the Community Guidelines, carry the required legal disclosures and a privacy notice covering data collection.
- Contests must be free to enter, must not ask entrants to transfer ownership of their submissions, and must not associate YouTube with the contest without written consent.
- Contests cannot be run through ad units.
- The page prohibits manipulating metrics such as views, likes or subscribers. It does not say in plain words whether asking for a subscribe as an entry condition counts. Treat subscribe-to-enter on YouTube as a risk and prefer a visit, a comment or your own entry page.

Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1620498

## TikTok (Community Guidelines, effective 13 September 2025, update announced for 24 September 2026)

TikTok has no single promotion page. Two sections apply.

- Integrity and Authenticity, "Deceptive Behaviors and Fake Engagement": TikTok does not allow accounts that mislead or manipulate the platform, or "the trade of services that artificially boost engagement or trick the recommendation system". Multiple accounts are allowed for legitimate purposes but not to deceive or break the rules. Automation tools and scripts are prohibited. Inauthentic likes and followers are removed, and "content that tries to manipulate people into giving gifts or inflating likes or follows isn't eligible for the FYF" (the For You feed).
- Regulated Goods, Services, and Commercial Activities: promoting your own business, product or service, or posting branded content for any incentive, requires the content disclosure setting. Undisclosed commercial content is ineligible for the For You feed and repeat failures can restrict or ban the account. Regulated and high-risk goods and services (gambling among them) cannot be traded, marketed or given access to, with limited exceptions for registered business accounts.

What it means: a giveaway post is commercial content, so use the disclosure setting. "Follow and like to enter" sits close to the fake-engagement line and can cost the post its For You reach. Prefer a comment, a question, or your own entry page, and never a mechanic that rewards mass follows or likes. Gambling-style prizes are out.

Sources: https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity and https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/regulated-commercial-activities

## Discord (Community Guidelines, effective 29 September 2025)

No section on giveaways or contests. The rules that bite: "Do not send unsolicited bulk messages (or spam)", no self-bots or user-bots (each account must belong to a human), no artificially inflating server membership or manipulating engagement metrics, and "Do not coordinate or participate in illegal gambling."

What it means: join-to-enter is fine, and about one campaign in five in the data asks for it. Do not mass-DM invites, do not reward bulk invites in a way that inflates membership, and keep raffles that cost money to enter off the server. Server rules on self-promotion apply on top.

Source: https://discord.com/guidelines

## Twitch (Community Guidelines, read 9 September 2026)

- Spam, Scams and Other Malicious Conduct: no viewership tampering "such as artificially inflating follow or live viewer stats", no cheating the Drops or channel points systems, no selling or sharing accounts, no unauthorized advertisements.
- Prohibited Gambling Content: no links or affiliate codes to sites with slots, roulette or dice games, and named sites (stake.com among them) cannot be streamed or linked. Skins gambling promotion is prohibited. Fantasy sports, sports betting and poker broadcasts are permitted.
- Breaking the Law: content that solicits illegal activity is prohibited.

What it means: follow-to-enter is common on Twitch (16% of campaigns in the data) and the guidelines target bots and fake follows, and asking viewers to follow is untouched. Paid "sub to enter" is not addressed by name. It had the lowest uptake of any action family in the data (median 0.05), so it costs entrants and gains little. Keep gambling sites and skins sites out of the prize and the chat.

Source: https://safety.twitch.tv/s/article/Community-Guidelines

## Telegram (Terms of Service)

Telegram has a built-in giveaway feature: "Channel admins can organize giveaways at their expense, purchasing a number of premium subscriptions to be distributed among present or future subscribers." Admins bear sole responsibility for complying with the laws governing giveaways in their jurisdiction, purchases are non-refundable, and channels that mislead users into joining through giveaways later refunded by third parties may be terminated or given a scam label. Spam and scams are prohibited.

What it means: join-to-enter is expected on Telegram. Legal compliance is entirely yours. Because so many crypto campaigns use Telegram, a plain product giveaway benefits from stating clearly what the prize is.

Source: https://telegram.org/tos

## Pinterest (Community Guidelines, updated May 2026, and Business help)

Pinterest no longer publishes a dedicated contest guideline. The Community Guidelines' spam rule applies: "Don't create or save content that is repetitive, deceptive, or irrelevant in an attempt to make money." The Business help page on promotions says Pinterest ads do not allow promotions that are sweepstakes, so a giveaway cannot be advertised through Pinterest ads.

What it means: organic pins about a giveaway are fine, incentivised mass saving reads as spam, and paid promotion of a sweepstakes on Pinterest is out.

Sources: https://policy.pinterest.com/en/community-guidelines and https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/promotions

## Reddit (Reddit Help, Spam, updated within three months of reading)

"Do not use Reddit for repeated or unsolicited mass engagement." Violations include mass-posting repetitive content for exposure or financial gain, mass-tagging or unsolicited messages, using tools that facilitate spam, and "leveraging multiple accounts to artificially inflate the subscriber count of a subreddit." To avoid being labelled a spammer: post authentic content in communities you take part in, be thoughtful about frequency if most of your posts link to a business you benefit from, and check each community's own rules, since moderators decide what counts as unwanted in their community. A separate rule prohibits deceptive interactions and manipulated content.

What it means: a giveaway post lives or dies on subreddit rules. Many ban giveaways outright, some have a giveaway thread or flair. Ask the moderators first, post once, and never ask for upvotes.

Sources: https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam and https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/41180423371156-Manipulated-Content-and-Misleading-Behavior

## LinkedIn (Professional Community Policies)

"You may not use LinkedIn to hold lotteries, contests, sweepstakes, or giveaways." The policies also prohibit artificially increasing engagement, agreeing with others in advance to like or reshare each other's content, and unwanted commercial or promotional messages.

What it means: LinkedIn is the one network that bans giveaways in its own policy. Announce a giveaway elsewhere and do not run entries through LinkedIn posts, comments or messages. A B2B giveaway runs on your own page with LinkedIn used only for a plain announcement, and even that sits close to the line.

Source: https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies

## Snapchat (Promotions Rules and Community Guidelines)

Promotions Rules: if you use Snapchat as part of any promotion, contest or sweepstakes you are responsible for legal compliance, you must expressly state in the official rules and make clear in the presentation that the promotion is in no way sponsored, endorsed, administered by or associated with Snap Inc., Snap is not responsible or liable to you, and you must not use Snap's names, marks or logos beyond what that statement requires, encourage illegal behaviour, or use spam tactics such as asking people to forward Snaps. Community Guidelines prohibit spam, "pay-for-follower promotions or other follower-growth schemes", and promoting gambling services.

What it means: no forward-to-enter, no paid follower schemes, and the Snap disclaimer in the rules.

Sources: https://help.snapchat.com/hc/articles/7047502545044 and https://values.snap.com/privacy/transparency/community-guidelines

## Steam (Online Conduct)

Steam's conduct rules list "running contests" alongside gambling, account selling and begging under prohibited commercial activity on Steam, and prohibit automatically generating accounts.

What it means: run the giveaway off Steam and send people to your own entry page. Game keys as prizes are fine to deliver. Do not run the contest inside Steam Community features.

Source: https://store.steampowered.com/online_conduct

## Bluesky (Community Guidelines, 19 September 2025)

No giveaway rule. Prohibited: spam, "artificially manipulate features or social signals to gain unearned reach", undisclosed commercial content, promoting gambling, and evading enforcement with new accounts.

What it means: disclose that a giveaway post is commercial, keep entry mechanics that do not inflate follower counts artificially, and avoid follow-for-follow schemes.

Source: https://bsky.social/about/support/community-guidelines

## Kick (Community Guidelines, read 9 September 2026)

Guideline 11, Gambling: "Gambling involving funds provided by other users, such as requiring buy-ins to participate in giveaways, is strictly prohibited." Creators must follow gambling laws in their jurisdiction, sponsored gambling is allowed only for licensed platforms with clear disclosure, and minors may not take part. Elsewhere: "The use of bots, engagement manipulation, or third-party tools to artificially inflate views, followers, or interactions is prohibited." Paid partnerships must use the Branded Content tool with a visual and verbal disclosure.

What it means: free entry only, never a buy-in, disclosure on sponsored giveaways, and no bought engagement.

Source: https://kick.com/community-guidelines

## Spotify (Spotify for Artists, Artificial Streaming)

"An artificial stream is a stream that doesn't reflect genuine user listening intent." Artificial streams earn no royalties, do not count toward public numbers or charts, and do not influence recommendations. Severe cases lead to removal or suspension. Any service that guarantees streams, followers or playlist placement for money is illegitimate.

What it means: a follow or a listen as an entry action is in a grey area. The policy targets intent and automation, so one follow from a real fan is fine and a mechanic that farms streams is not. Prefer "save the album" or "follow" as optional actions, never paid or repeatable streams.

Source: https://artists.spotify.com/artificial-streaming

## Threads (Threads Terms of Use)

The Threads Terms "supplement and amend the Instagram Terms of Use and the Meta Community Standards, which are incorporated into these Terms by reference." Threads publishes no separate promotion rules.

What it means (an inference from the terms, since no page says it in so many words): treat Instagram's promotion guidelines as applying to Threads. Release Meta, state that the promotion is not sponsored by Threads or Instagram, and keep tags accurate.

Source: https://help.instagram.com/769983657850450
