# Illustrative Prize patterns

These examples are invented illustrations and describe no real campaign or business. Business types, offers and values were created for this guide. Values are round illustrative amounts in USD, not observed prices or benchmarks. None of the examples predicts participation or results.

**Pattern** names the Prize decision. **Advice** explains how to adapt it. Keep the advertised retail value separate from the cost of buying, delivering or servicing the Prize.

## A year of a consumable

**Pattern:** A fountain-pen ink maker offers a year of ink refills with an illustrative retail value of 200 USD.

**Advice:** Define the refill quantity and delivery schedule. Budget the full year's fulfillment, including postage. The Prize connects the recurring supply to the product the audience already uses.

## Store credit

**Pattern:** A yarn shop offers 300 USD of credit for a knitting project chosen by the recipient.

**Advice:** Let the recipient choose colours and materials. State whether shipping is covered and whether the credit can be used across several orders. Calculate the cost from the goods likely to be redeemed.

## A complementary partner bundle

**Pattern:** A seed merchant and a plant-label maker supply a seed-starting bundle with an illustrative combined retail value of 100 USD.

**Advice:** Give each partner a written list of what it supplies and who ships the bundle. Keep every part useful to the same audience. Include handling and coordination in the budget even when partners supply the goods.

## Many small units plus a hero item

**Pattern:** A calligraphy supplier pairs a 600 USD lettering desk with a pool of 20 USD practice kits.

**Advice:** Use the desk to explain the main offer and the kits to reward more people. Choose the kit quantity only after calculating packing and delivery costs. State each tier separately in the terms.

## Service capacity

**Pattern:** A watch-repair workshop offers a servicing package with an illustrative retail value of 400 USD.

**Advice:** Reserve the labour before announcing the Prize. Define the eligible watch types, parts allowance and transport arrangements so the promised service fits the workshop's capacity.

## Partner-supplied tickets

**Pattern:** An astronomy club arranges a planetarium visit through a venue partner, with an illustrative ticket value of 100 USD.

**Advice:** Confirm the ticket allocation and booking window with the venue. State whether transport and guest admission are covered. Partner-supplied tickets can still bring administration and travel costs.

## Equipment plus instruction

**Pattern:** A stained-glass tutor offers a beginner tool set and an introductory course with an illustrative combined retail value of 800 USD.

**Advice:** Match the equipment to the course requirements. State the skill level, safety requirements and booking limits before entry. Price the tools and teaching time separately in the budget.

## A membership with defined limits

**Pattern:** A language-practice app offers a premium membership with an illustrative retail value of 200 USD.

**Advice:** Define the membership term, included features and any renewal arrangements. Check support costs and access restrictions before calling the Prize inexpensive to deliver.

## Community-specific collectibles

**Pattern:** A puzzle magazine offers a signed art print and a custom puzzle book with an illustrative combined retail value of 100 USD.

**Advice:** Explain what makes the items distinctive to existing readers. Confirm production and shipping before using scarcity in the offer.

## Digital access

**Pattern:** A digital embroidery designer offers access to a pattern library with an illustrative retail value of 300 USD.

**Advice:** State the access term, file formats and licence limits. Make clear whether the recipient needs software or equipment to use the files.

## An own-product starter bundle

**Pattern:** A leathercraft supplier offers a beginner materials kit with an illustrative retail value of 200 USD.

**Advice:** Include enough material to finish a defined project. List any tools the recipient must already own so the Prize remains useful at the advertised value.

## Cash as an explicit tradeoff

**Pattern:** A personal-budgeting newsletter considers a 300 USD cash Prize.

**Advice:** Cash is useful beyond the newsletter's subject. Decide whether that broad interest serves the objective, and compare it with a Prize tied to the reader's budgeting needs. Include payment handling in the plan.

## Offers excluded from these illustrations

- Token airdrops, NFT mints and similar crypto offers require a separate discussion when explicitly requested.
- Regulated goods are outside this example library.
- Discount codes and coupons with a purchase condition are not recommended as headline Prizes. Chance-to-purchase raffles, including sneaker raffles, offer the right to buy an item. Purchase-only offers belong to the purchase-opportunity segment and are excluded from ordinary campaign benchmarks.
